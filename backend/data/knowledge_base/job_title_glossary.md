# Medics Online — Job Title Glossary

Medics Online places two broad professions: **Doctors** and **Nurses**. Dr. Eezy must always
distinguish between them, since qualifications, registration bodies, and typical shift
patterns differ.

## Doctors (medical practitioners)
Doctors hold a medical degree (MBChB or equivalent) and are registered with the HPCSA
(Health Professions Council of South Africa). Common specialties on Medics Online:

- **General Practitioner (GP)** — primary care, outpatient consultations, referrals.
- **Cardiologist** — heart and cardiovascular system specialist.
- **Pediatrician** — specializes in infants, children, and adolescents.
- **Orthopedic Surgeon** — surgical specialist for bones, joints, and muscles.
- **Anesthesiologist** — manages anesthesia and critical care during surgery.

Doctors typically work day shifts with on-call or theatre rotations depending on specialty.

## Nurses
Nurses are registered with SANC (South African Nursing Council). Common specialties:

- **Registered Nurse – ICU** — critical/intensive care, ventilator management.
- **Registered Nurse – Pediatric Ward** — nursing care for children.
- **Theatre Nurse (Scrub Nurse)** — assists surgeons in the operating theatre.
- **Midwife** — labour, delivery, antenatal and postnatal care.
- **Nurse Practitioner** — advanced practice nurse who can run independent primary
  care consultations.

Nurses typically work 12-hour rotating shifts, including night shifts, unlike most doctors.

## Why the distinction matters for matching and rostering
- A candidate's CV should be matched only against jobs in the **same profession**
  (Doctor CVs matched to Doctor jobs, Nurse CVs matched to Nurse jobs) unless they
  explicitly hold both qualifications.
- When generating a roster, doctors and nurses are scheduled under **different rules**
  (see `roster_policy.md`) — they should never be pooled into the same shift-fairness
  calculation.
